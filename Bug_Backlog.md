# Bug Backlog

Open defects and known risks. **P1 items are surfaced at every `/startup`.**

Rows are ordered by priority, highest first.

Priorities:

- **P1** — blocks the daily pipeline. Data is being lost, the ring isn't syncing, or the
  dashboard is wrong. Fix before new features.
- **P2** — degrades something that still works. Fix when convenient.
- **P3** — annoyance, cosmetic, or cleanup. Fix when idle.
- **RISK** — not a defect yet. A known failure mode that will become P1 if unhandled.

Entry format: one row per item. Add the date found; add the date closed and move the row
to Closed rather than deleting it — a fixed bug is video material.

| R-004 | **P1** | 2026-08-02 | data durability | **Escalated 2026-08-13: there is now real data to lose.** `ring.db` holds irreplaceable readings and lives on a decade-old SSD with no backup, no rotation, and no verified restore. A nightly `cp` is not enough on its own — use `sqlite3 .backup` (safe against a live WAL connection), keep dated copies off the hub, and **actually restore one** before calling it done. An untested backup is a belief, not a backup. |

| R-015 | P2 | 2026-08-16 | hub / sync | **`sync.py` leaves `ring_clock_utc` and `clock_offset_s` null.** Both columns exist in `sync_runs` specifically to make R-002 measurable, and the sync service never populates them — confirmed in a real run (`"ring_clock_utc":null,"clock_offset_s":null`). The dashboard's status line consequently reports "clock offset unmeasured" forever. `parse_hr_log_ring_timestamp` gives the echoed request timestamp rather than the ring's own clock, so this needs a different source; until it has one, drift on this ring is unknown and unknowable from the store. |
| R-002 | RISK | 2026-08-02 | protocol / time | **Clock drift is unmeasured and there is no obvious source for it.** RTC set to UTC 2026-08-09T07:07:01Z (previously never set). Storage is UTC ISO-8601 everywhere, so the ring↔hub offset must be captured at sync time or the store silently fills with plausible wrong times. **Correction 2026-08-09:** the 4-byte timestamp in HR frame `sub_type` 1 *echoes the requested day start* — it confirms the ring parsed the request but reveals nothing about the ring's own clock, so it cannot fill `sync_runs.clock_offset_s`. No read-time command is known. Measure drift instead by comparing when samples appear against events of known wall-clock time, and re-check after several days. |
| R-003 | RISK | 2026-08-02 | hub / radio | The 2014 MacBook Air's Broadcom chip shares one 2.4 GHz radio between WiFi and Bluetooth. On a 2.4 GHz SSID, BLE scanning degrades intermittently — the hardest failure mode to diagnose later. Keep the hub on 5 GHz. |
| R-005 | RISK | 2026-08-02 | hub / systemd | The Bluetooth stack initializes ~43 s into boot (USB device enumerates at ~2.8 s, `Bluetooth: Core ver 2.22` at 43.0 s). A sync service starting earlier will find no adapter. Symptom if unhandled: syncing works by hand but never after a reboot. `ring-sync.service` needs `After=bluetooth.target` and must tolerate an adapter that isn't ready yet. |
| R-008 | RISK | 2026-08-08 | protocol / sleep | **Sleep is the top-priority metric and has no public Python reference.** colmi_r02_client has no `sleep.py` at all; Gadgetbridge's Colmi/Yawell classes are the only prior art. It may also ride the second vendor service (`de5bf728`) rather than the command channel, which nothing upstream documents. Budget session 6 as reverse-engineering, not porting — it is the largest genuinely unsolved piece of the protocol. |
| R-009 | RISK | 2026-08-08 | hub / battery | **No low-battery warning; battery is now recorded but not alerted on.** The R06 ran 80% → 1% unnoticed once already — that job belonged to the vendor app, which this ring will never run. Resolved in part 2026-08-16: `hub/sync.py` stores a `battery` sample on every run and the dashboard shows it, turning orange below 30%. What is still missing is anything that reaches you when you are not looking at the dashboard. At ~5.5 days per charge a charging habit is the practical answer; real push needs an installed PWA over HTTPS, blocked behind R-001. |
| R-010 | RISK | 2026-08-08 | hub / radio range | **Body-worn costs ~21 dB.** Measured at the same desk and the same battery level: −60 dBm in the charging case, **−81 dBm on the finger**. Human tissue absorbs 2.4 GHz heavily. If −81 is the figure beside the hub, range from another room may not support a reliable sync — and the shelved adaptive-policy thresholds (−85 present / −80 connect) were evidently calibrated against a ring on a table, not a hand. Measure RSSI at realistic distances (desk, next room, bedside) before designing the scheduler; the answer may dictate where the hub physically lives. |
| R-012 | RISK | 2026-08-08 | hub / battery | **Idle draw dominates; PPG is the minority term.** Measured 100% → 18% over 4.75 days at HR=30min = **~17–18%/day**, giving the ring a **~5.5-day life**. Factory config with HR logging *disabled* was ~13%/day, so enabling HR every 30 min costs only **~5%/day** — the other ~13%/day is a floor that PPG scheduling cannot touch. Halving the HR interval would buy back at most ~2.5%/day, which is not where the win is. **The win is finding what burns 13%/day while nominally idle → R-014.** Lesson recorded: the earlier 6.7%/day figure came from the first 14 h of a full charge and was wrong by ~3x. This gauge is heavily non-linear at the top (0.28%/h for the first 14 h, 0.78%/h thereafter) — never estimate drain from day one of a charge. |
| R-014 | RISK | 2026-08-09 | protocol / battery | **Unqueried sensing flags.** Only the HR logging settings (`0x16`) have ever been read. SpO2, stress, HRV and temperature auto-monitoring plausibly have their own enable flags, and if they ship on by default the ring is spending power on metrics `PLAN.md` explicitly deprioritizes. Circumstantial evidence: drain roughly halved after enabling HR logging, which only makes sense if something else was running before. Finding these is the highest-value protocol work left after sleep. |

| R-006 | RISK | 2026-08-02 | 2026-08-13 | "bleak 3.0.2 will conflict with colmi_r02_client." **Moot — it was never installed and never will be.** The reference is MIT, so layouts are lifted into `protocol/` with attribution and `requirements.txt` pins only bleak, fastapi and uvicorn. | R-011 | RISK | 2026-08-08 | 2026-08-09 | "Settings writes may clobber the undocumented byte[4]." **Disproven.** `set_hr_log_settings` sends only bytes 2 and 3, and byte[4] survived unchanged across an enable write (`16 01 02 1e 05 .. 3c` → `16 01 01 1e 05 .. 3b`). The field's meaning is still unknown and remains noted in `parse_hr_log_settings`, but it is not at risk. Also learned: writes ack with `16 02 01 ..`, so acceptance can be confirmed rather than assumed. |
| R-007 | RISK | 2026-08-02 | 2026-08-08 | "Ring unreachable over BLE while charging." **Not reproducible — the premise was wrong.** In a powered case beside the hub the ring scans at −61 dBm, connects, and completes a battery round trip normally. Session 2's timeout had another cause: most likely an unpowered case, greater distance, or a battery already too flat to hold a link (it read 1% six days later). Resolving this also settled `parse_battery`'s `is_charging` flag — `byte[2]` is 1 on the charger, 0 on the finger. Lesson kept: a single failed connect is not evidence of a mechanism. |
